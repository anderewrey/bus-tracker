Guide

From the download to your first alarm, in about ten minutes.

1. Install 2. Log in 3. Let it notify you 4. Draw your zone 5. When it watches 6. Skip one time 7. When it stops 8. The alarm 9. BusPlanner page 10. Something's off?

1. Install

Bus Tracker isn't on the Play Store, so you install it from its download, and Android asks a few more questions than usual. That's expected.

  1. On your phone, tap Download, then open bus-tracker.apk.
  2. The first time, Android says your browser isn't allowed to install apps. Tap Settings, turn on Allow from this source, and go back.
  3. Google Play Protect doesn't know the app, so it suggests scanning it. I'd tap Scan app: Google checks it, then the install goes on. Install without scanning works too.

Updates: when a new version is out, a card at the top of the list says so, with a Download button. Install it over the old one, the same way; your login, zones and settings stay. To know, the app asks GitHub once a day, and sends nothing about you.

2. Log in

Use the same account you use on your school board's BusPlanner website.

  1. Portal address: the address of that website, e.g. example.mybusplanner.ca.
  2. Your Email and Password, then Log in.

The app reads your buses, stops and times, and shows one card per bus and direction: the pickup in the morning, the dropoff in the afternoon. If two of your kids ride the same bus, it's one card.

3. Let it notify you

When the app asks to send notifications, say yes: without them, nothing can ring.

If your phone is often on silent or in Do Not Disturb, go to Settings › Quiet mode override. The alarm then rings like your morning alarm, at the alarm volume, even in Do Not Disturb.

4. Draw your zone

The zone is where the bus has to be for your phone to ring. Draw it somewhere around the neighbourhood, far enough to give you time to get boots on.

  1. Tap a bus card to open its page, then under Main zone, tap Draw new.
  2. Tap the map to place each corner. Undo removes the last one; drag a dot to move it.
  3. Tap Done to close the shape, give it a name, and Save.

One zone can serve several cards: if the afternoon stop is inside it, the afternoon gets it too. To use a zone from another card, tap Choose. Test alarm lets you hear what it will sound like.

A heads-up, if you want one: under Heads-up zone, draw a second zone farther along the route. When the bus enters it, your phone makes a sound, once, a few minutes before the alarm. If the bus sometimes crosses your main zone on another part of its route before coming back, tick Alarm only after the heads-up: the alarm then waits for the bus to come through the heads-up first. Draw that one long enough (500 m or more) so the bus is seen in it.

5. When it watches

  • Watching period: on a card's page, − and + set how long before and after the bus's time the app watches. It starts with 30 minutes on each side.
  • Automatic start: the switch at the top of the list. On, every card starts watching by itself at the start of its period.
  • Days: tap Automatic start › to choose the days, for every card at once. A card can also have its own days, on its page.
  • Holidays, snow days: turn Automatic start off and pick Off until… a date. That morning, it comes back on by itself.

Changes apply right away: remove today from the days while it's watching, and it stops.

6. Skip one time

Each card has a bell. It's only about one period: the one going on, or else the next one.

  • Nobody's taking the bus this morning? Turn off the morning bell. The afternoon stays as usual, and tomorrow the bell is back on by itself.
  • Turned it off by mistake? Turn it back on before the period ends and it watches again.
  • Stop watching in the notification does the same as turning the bell off.

7. When it stops

On a card's page, End of watching says when it's done for that trip:

  • Bus parked at the school, the usual for the morning.
  • Bus leaves a zone, the usual for the afternoon: once the bus has left your zone, the kids are off.
  • Bus enters a zone, or Only at the end of the period.

Whatever you pick, it always stops at the end of the period, which a delay posted by BusPlanner pushes back.

8. The alarm

  • When the bus enters your zone, the phone rings once, for a minute at most.
  • Silence, a volume key or a swipe stops the sound. The app keeps watching.
  • In Settings: the Sound, Vibration, Test alarm, and the same for BusPlanner's late or cancelled notices.

9. BusPlanner page

BusPlanner ›, at the top of the list:

  • Reload reads your buses, stops and times again. It also does it by itself once a day.
  • When something changed (a new stop, a new time, a new bus), a red card says Bus schedule changed. For a moved stop or a new bus, you choose whether your zones carry over.
  • A delay or a cancellation posted by BusPlanner shows in red on that bus's card, all day.
  • Log out is there too. Your zones and settings stay on the phone.

10. Something's off?

  • Look at the card's status line: the distance and the street. The line under it, GPS, is the time the bus last reported. "updated 5 min ago" in red means the position is old.
  • No vehicle means BusPlanner has no bus assigned right now; there's nothing to follow.
  • To tell me about a problem: Settings › Open Debug › Export saves the app's log as a file. Then open an issue on GitHub and describe what happened. The log has your bus numbers and streets, so look it over before you attach it.
