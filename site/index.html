Bus Tracker

Your phone watches the school bus, so you don't have to.

For Android, if your school board uses BusPlanner and you have a parent account on it.

Download Guide

Here's what a school day looks like ↓

7:03 AM

It starts on its own.

A little before the bus is due, on school days, the app starts watching. You don't have to open it or remember anything.

7:12 AM

Glance at where the bus is.

The notification tells you how far it is and which street it's on. The app and the map also show when its GPS last reported. Open the map if you want to see it move.

7:30 AM

The bus gets close. Your phone rings.

You draw the zone yourself, somewhere around the neighbourhood: far enough to give you time to get boots on. It rings once, for a minute at most.

Another morning

The bus is running late.

When BusPlanner posts a delay or a cancellation, the app lets you know, and it stays in red on that bus for the rest of the day.

Another morning

Nobody's taking the bus this morning.

Sick day? Turn off this morning's bell. The afternoon stays as usual, and tomorrow it's back on by itself.

A snow day, a long weekend

No school until Tuesday.

Turn Automatic start off until the day school is back. That morning, it picks up again without you.

Any day

The schedule changed.

New stop, new time, new bus: the app checks BusPlanner every day, tells you what changed, and asks before touching your zones.

2:09 PM

Same thing on the way home.

The afternoon bus gets watched the same way, with its own zone if you want one.

Questions you might have

What does it know?

Only what it reads from BusPlanner: the bus, where your stop is, the times the bus is expected, the school, and where the bus is while it's watching. No names.

Does it know where I am?

No. It never asks for your phone's location. Your zone is just a shape you draw on the map, somewhere around the neighbourhood.

What about my BusPlanner password?

It stays encrypted on your phone, and only goes to your own BusPlanner portal, the same way your browser would send it.

Will it kill my battery?

I haven't noticed any difference on mine. It only works while it's watching, and the rest of the day it uses next to nothing.

My phone is on silent. Will it still ring?

If you want it to. Turn on Quiet mode override and it rings like your morning alarm, even with Do Not Disturb on.

What if the bus stops sending its position?

It happens. When the last position gets more than two minutes old, the app says so ("updated 5 min ago", in red on the screen) instead of passing it off as live. If it looks stuck, it logs in to BusPlanner again in case the problem is on its side.

What if my phone restarts?

It picks up where it was, and the next mornings start as usual. If the phone happens to be off right when watching should start, just open the app and it starts.

Can I count on it?

I count on it every school day, but it's only as good as what the bus sends. It saves you from refreshing a web page for twenty minutes; it doesn't replace keeping an eye on the time.

Who made this?

A parent who got tired of keeping the BusPlanner page open and checking it every few minutes. I wanted my phone to do the watching on its own and just tell me when it matters. It's free today, no ads, no sign-up besides your BusPlanner account, and the code is open for anyone to read.

Will it stay free?

That's the plan. But Google is changing the rules for apps installed outside the Play Store, and keeping Bus Tracker easy to install may end up costing something. If it does, I may ask for a small fee to cover it, and I'll say so here first.

What do I need?

An Android phone (Android 10 or newer), and a parent account on your school board's BusPlanner portal.
